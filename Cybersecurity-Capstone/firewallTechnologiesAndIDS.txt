Firewall Technologies and IDS classification

Firewall technologies are used to segment large networks into 
smaller subnetworks, contain threats, and prevent threats. The 
purpose of all of these functions is to stop threats before they can 
reach the center of the network. There are several different types of 
firewall technologies including Packet Filter Firewalls, 
Application-Level Proxy Servers, Stateful Packet Inspection 
Firewalls, and DMZ Firewalls. Packet filter firewalls filter packets 
by the address of incomming traffic and rejects anything that does 
not match the determined list of trusted sources. It uses rules based 
on information in packets like TCP/IP port numbers, source and 
destination addresses, or data type, to determine what traffic to 
block. Application-level proxy servers check traffic by examining the 
application used for each individual packet to verify authenticity. 
Stateful packet inspection firewalls are among the most secure of 
the firewall technologies. These firewalls look at all parts of a 
packet to determine what traffic to deny, it rejects any data that 
was not requested by the network. There is a second level of 
screening that determines the state of each packet, this is the 
source of the name of the technology.

Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are used to monitor networks and 
systems for malicious activity, policy violations, or otherwise 
suspicious activity, in order to prevent potential threats. This 
suspicious activity is typically reported to an administrator who 
deals with the potential threat. Activity information gathered by an 
IDS can aslo be collected by a SIEM (security information and event 
management) system in order to determine the validity of a threat vs 
a false positive. The most common classification for IDS are Network 
IDS (NIDS) and Host-Based IDS (HIDS). NIDS monitor and analyze 
incoming network traffic whereas HIDS monitor specific files. IDS 
have several methods of threat detection, these include: 
Signature-Based Detection which recognizes known patterns like 
exploitation attempts, Anomaly-Based Detection, which recognizes 
anomalous/suspicious activity and records it as potentially risky, 
and Reputation-Based Detection which recognizes protential threats 
according to ruputation scores.
