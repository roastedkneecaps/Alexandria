Notes Abstract (from discussion):

Baselining normal traffic signatures is the process of monitoring and recording behavior that is typical on 
the network overtime to establish a reference point for what activity should look like. Any deviation from 
the 'normal' established from baseilning could be indicative of malicious activity.

Malicious activity falls into several categories:
 - Scanning/Reconnaissance - repeated connection attempts to multiple ports, this is seen during port scans 
and 
network mapping
 - Denial-of-Service - excessive traffic (sent packages) that are meant to overwhelm a network and occupy 
resources, this takes two forms DoS (Denial-of-Service) and DDoS (Distributed-Denial-of-Service), DoS attacks 
involve only one attacker machine, DDoS attacks involve many, either physical or virtual
 - Command-and-Control or Malware - small, periodic outbound traffic to known malicious domains or ports
 - Data Exfiltration - outbound traffic that is typically made up of encrypted payloads, large file 
transfers, and small periodic file transfers (e.g. nightlys designed to avoid detection)
 - Spoofing - traffic with forged IP headers or unusual protocol usages

Attack Signature Analysis Techniques:
The general process for network traffic signature analysis is as follows:
 - Packet Capture - traffic is captured on an interface or port
 - Preprocessing - captured packets are procesed, this cleans and normalizes the data
 - Signature Matching - packets are compred against known signatures

Factors to keep in mind: 
 - Since signature analysis relies on known attack vectors, it does not catch certain attacks such as well 
obfuscated attacks or zero-day exploits
 - False Negatives are always possible due to the ever-evolving number and sophistication of attacks
 - False Positives are also possible, these don't pose a security risk as much as they clog resources which 
can make a network or organization more suceptable to attacks
